Both models agreed that our topic was relevant, the video was organized, and we explained both the benefits and risks of AI coding assistants. They also recognized that we used GitHub branches and pull requests to collaborate.

The main difference was the grading. Gemini gave us **85/100**, while ChatGPT gave us **51/100**, which is a difference of **34 points**. ChatGPT was stricter and only gave credit for what was available at that stage.

I found ChatGPT’s feedback more useful because it clearly explained what was completed and what was still missing.

## A4. Do You Agree With the AI’s Grade?

I do not completely agree with either grade. Gemini’s score was too high because it gave us credit for work that had not been completed. ChatGPT’s score felt low when considering the quality of our research, video, and GitHub collaboration, but it was more accurate based on what was available at the time.

I did not agree with Gemini’s suggestion that every member needed a speaking role. Our professor confirmed that our division of responsibilities was acceptable. Two members handled the research and video, while the third handled the AI evaluations and reflection.

The grading process was confusing because we needed both AI evaluations before we could complete the comparison and reflection. The 34-point difference also showed how differently AI models can judge the same project.

## A5. What Would You Do Differently?

If we redid the project, we would organize the repository earlier and save our AI prompts and responses from the beginning. We would also add better references, direct links to all project files, team responsibilities, and the final presentation file in the README.

We would keep the same division of responsibilities because it allowed every member to contribute to an important part of the project. I also do not think the video needed to be longer because it was meant to be a short presentation.

# Part B — AI Research Assistance Reflection

## B1. How We Used AI During Research

We used **Gemini and ChatGPT** to brainstorm ideas, organize our research, simplify technical explanations, and improve the wording in our presentation. We also used both models to evaluate the project using the same rubric.

## B2. What Worked Well

AI was most useful for organizing our topic into clear sections, including the history, tools, benefits, risks, and future impact of coding assistants.

For example, we asked AI to explain how coding tools developed from basic autocomplete into modern agentic systems. The timeline it provided helped us structure that part of the video.

## B3. What Did Not Work Well

AI sometimes made broad claims about productivity and code quality without providing strong evidence. We had to review and rewrite those statements more carefully.

Gemini’s grading was another example. It gave high scores for the AI Evaluation Setup and Reflection Quality even though those parts were not completed. It also suggested that every member needed to speak without knowing that our professor had approved our division of responsibilities. This showed us that AI can make assumptions when it does not have enough context.

## B4. Overall Reflection

AI helped us work faster by supporting our brainstorming, organization, and writing. However, we still had to check the information and decide whether its suggestions were accurate and useful.

For a similar project, we would still use AI, but we would save our prompts and responses from the beginning and verify important claims using reliable sources. The biggest lesson was that AI can sound confident while still being incorrect or making assumptions, so human judgment is always necessary.
